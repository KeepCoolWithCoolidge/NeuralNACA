    FAQ¶

    How can a model trained on 0.05 % of NeuralFoil's data be more accurate? Mostly because of geometry, and partly because of specialisation, and not because it is a better XFOIL emulator.

    • Geometry. NeuralFoil does not see the NACA section itself: it analyses an 8+8-weight Kulfan (CST) fit of it. The fit is geometrically excellent (shape errors of hundredths of a percent of chord), but XFOIL's transition prediction is sensitive enough that the fitted shape gives a noticeably different answer. That difference is about as large as NeuralFoil's entire error against the exact section. Scored against XFOIL on its own fitted shape, NeuralFoil is more accurate than NeuralNACA. Even a plain MLP without physics, trained on 4,096 exact-geometry XFOIL runs, beats NeuralFoil on NACA sections.
    • Specialisation. NeuralFoil must represent every airfoil shape, ±180° of incidence, Reynolds numbers from 10² to 10¹⁰ and arbitrary transition settings with one network. NeuralNACA covers one three-parameter family in a bounded box at one transition setting.
    • Physics. The panel-method backbone lowers the error by 17-29 % relative to the same model without it.

    See the ground-truth study and the ablation. The comparison says nothing about which tool is better in general.

    Is this a physics-informed neural network (PINN)? In the broad sense, yes: physics is built into the architecture (panel-method baseline and features, exact symmetry, drag prior). In the narrow sense of a PDE-residual loss, no. That option is implemented, but it does not help here (see Method and the ablation). Of the built-in ingredients only the panel method measurably improves accuracy; the symmetry and the drag prior make the model consistent rather than more accurate.

    Is the comparison fair to NeuralFoil? We tried to make it as fair as we could, and to make every choice checkable.

    • Both models get the same exact coordinates and conditions and are scored on the same converged points.
    • The XFOIL settings match NeuralFoil's data pipeline: 279 panels, \(N_\mathrm{crit} = 9\), 100 iterations.
    • All eight NeuralFoil sizes are reported.
    • The comparison is repeated under five definitions of the XFOIL ground truth, including NeuralFoil's own warm-started protocol on its own Kulfan geometry.

    The remaining asymmetry is intrinsic: we chose the family and the domain. See the threats-to-validity table.

    Why a cold start for every XFOIL point? So that a label does not depend on which other points were solved, or in what order. This makes data sets order-independent, parallelisable and extensible (the Sobol prefixes of the data-efficiency curve rely on it). The cost is that near stall a cold start can land on a different solution branch than a warm-started sweep. The ground-truth study measures how much that matters.

    Why 279 panels? It is AeroSandbox's default re-panelling, which NeuralFoil's training data used. Evaluating against XFOIL at 160 panels (XFOIL's default) would make NeuralFoil look about 10 % worse, which would be unfair to it.

    Can I use it for NACA 5-digit / 6-series / my own airfoil? Not with the shipped model. The pipeline is generic in principle: replace the geometry, panel nodes and features, and retrain. The package does not do this for you. For arbitrary shapes, use NeuralFoil.

    Can I get gradients for optimisation? Yes. Everything from the geometry parameters to the outputs is a PyTorch graph; see Predictions → Gradients. The shipped weights are smooth, but keep the support flag in mind: an optimiser will happily exploit unsupported regions.

    How fast is it? See Cost per prediction. It is far faster than XFOIL. Compared with NeuralFoil it depends on the batch shape: NeuralNACA needs one small panel solve per distinct airfoil, so many angles or Reynolds numbers on the same section are very cheap.

    The numbers I get differ in the last digits. On another platform, retraining changes the weights slightly; see Reproducing the results. Predictions from the shipped weights should agree to about 10⁻⁶. If they do not, please open an issue with the output of neuralnaca info and neuralnaca reproduce --level 0 --json report.json.

    Why is CD sometimes enormous? You are almost certainly outside the training support (deep stall of a thin section at low Reynolds number, where XFOIL itself does not converge). Check supported; an UnsupportedWarning should have been raised. See Limitations.
