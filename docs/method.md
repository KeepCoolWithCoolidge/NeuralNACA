# Method

NeuralNACA approximates the map

\[
(m, p, t, \mathrm{Re}, \alpha) \;\longmapsto\; (C_L,\; \ln C_D,\; C_M)
\]

that XFOIL 6.99 defines for NACA 4-digit sections (free transition, \(N_\mathrm{crit} = 9\), Mach 0). It is a
*physics-embedded* neural network: classical aerodynamics supplies a baseline and the structure of the model, and an
ensemble of small neural networks learns the viscous correction from XFOIL data.

!!! info "Is this a PINN?"
    Not in the narrow sense of a network trained to satisfy a PDE residual. The physics enters through (1) a
    differentiable potential-flow panel solution used as baseline and as input features, (2) an exactly enforced
    reflection symmetry, and (3) a flat-plate skin-friction prior. An optional autograd-residual loss (the classic PINN
    ingredient) is implemented but **off by default**, because the ablation study finds it does not improve accuracy.
    The [ablation](results.md#ablation-what-each-physics-ingredient-contributes) quantifies what each ingredient
    contributes: the panel method improves accuracy markedly, while the symmetry and the drag prior mainly guarantee
    consistency.

## 1. Geometry

The NACA 4-digit section with maximum camber \(m\) at chordwise position \(p\) and thickness \(t\) has the half
thickness

\[
y_t = 5t\,(0.2969\sqrt{x} - 0.1260x - 0.3516x^2 + 0.2843x^3 - 0.1015x^4)
\]

(the classical open-trailing-edge form, as generated by XFOIL's `NACA` command) applied perpendicular to the camber
line \(y_c = \tfrac{m}{p^2}(2px - x^2)\) for \(x < p\) and \(\tfrac{m}{(1-p)^2}((1-2p) + 2px - x^2)\) otherwise.
`neuralnaca.naca4_coordinates` produces the coordinates given to XFOIL and NeuralFoil (100 points per side, cosine
spacing); the panel method uses the same formula in PyTorch.

## 2. Inviscid baseline: a differentiable Hess-Smith panel method

The section is discretised into \(N = 120\) straight panels (cosine spacing). Each panel carries a constant source
strength \(\sigma_j\), and all panels share one vortex strength \(\gamma\). Flow tangency at the \(N\) panel midpoints
and the Kutta condition (equal tangential velocity on the two trailing-edge panels) give \(N+1\) linear equations.

Potential flow is linear in the free-stream vector, so the system is solved once for a unit free stream along \(x\) and
once along \(y\). The lift then follows from the Kutta-Joukowski theorem (\(C_L = 2\Gamma/V_\infty c\)) and the
quarter-chord moment from integrating \(C_p = 1 - V_t^2/V_\infty^2\). Both are closed-form functions of \(\alpha\):

\[
C_{L,\mathrm{inv}}(\alpha) = A_L\cos\alpha + B_L\sin\alpha, \qquad
C_{M,\mathrm{inv}}(\alpha) = A_M - \left(B_M\cos^2\alpha + C_M\cos\alpha\sin\alpha + D_M\sin^2\alpha\right).
\]

The six constants depend only on the geometry. They are computed once per airfoil (`neuralnaca.panel.airfoil_constants`,
a batched dense solve), and the network evaluates the closed form, so \(\partial C_{L,\mathrm{inv}}/\partial\alpha\) is
exact.

**Validation (test suite, `tests/test_panel.py`).**

* On Joukowski airfoils the lift converges monotonically to the exact conformal-mapping solution under refinement.
  Convergence is slow, as expected for constant-source panels at a cusped trailing edge.
* For closed-trailing-edge NACA sections, successive refinements change \(C_L\) by less than 0.1 %. At 960 panels
  the result agrees with XFOIL's inviscid lift for NACA 4412 to 0.2 % (XFOIL closes the trailing-edge gap with its own
  panel).
* At the 120 panels used by the model, inviscid \(C_L\) is within 2 % (relative) and \(C_M\) within 0.0065 of XFOIL's
  own inviscid solution for seven sections and four angles.
* Lift and moment satisfy the reflection identities to round-off. Lift from circulation agrees with lift from
  integrated pressure. The lift slope matches \(2\pi(1 + 0.77t)\) for NACA 0012, and the zero-lift angle of NACA
  2412 matches thin-airfoil theory.

!!! note "Known property: open trailing edge"
    The NACA definition leaves a small trailing-edge gap (0.25 % of \(t\)). With the Kutta condition applied across
    that gap, the discrete solution drifts slowly under refinement (about −0.7 % in \(C_L\) per doubling) instead of
    converging; XFOIL avoids this by closing the gap with a source panel. The model uses a **fixed** 120-panel
    discretisation, so the inviscid quantities are deterministic features within 2 % of XFOIL's inviscid answer, and
    the network learns the remaining difference. A test pins the size of the drift. A trailing-edge gap panel is a
    candidate improvement for a future feature version (it would require retraining).

## 3. Drag prior

\(\ln C_D\) is predicted as a correction to a flat-plate turbulent skin-friction estimate with a thickness form
factor:

\[
C_{D,0} = 2\,C_f\,(1 + 2t + 60t^4), \qquad C_f = \frac{0.455}{(\log_{10}\mathrm{Re})^{2.58}} \quad
\text{(Prandtl-Schlichting)}.
\]

It captures the dominant Reynolds-number and thickness dependence of profile drag, so the network only has to learn
the (smaller) laminar-run, separation and lift-dependent parts.

## 4. Features

| # | feature | sign under reflection |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | \(m / 0.04\) | − |
| 2 | \((p_e - 0.45)/0.25\) with gated position \(p_e = 0.45 + (p - 0.45)\,[1 - e^{-(m/0.01)^2}]\) | + |
| 3 | \((t - 0.12)/0.06\) | + |
| 4 | \((\ln\mathrm{Re} - \ln 10^6)/1.2\) | + |
| 5 | \(\alpha / 10^\circ\) | − |
| 6 | \(C_{L,\mathrm{inv}}(\alpha)\) | − |
| 7 | \(10\,C_{M,\mathrm{inv}}(\alpha)\) | − |

The camber-position gate makes \(p\) irrelevant for symmetric sections (whose \(p\) is undefined). The gate must be an
**even** function of \(m\): a reflected section has the same camber position. An earlier pre-release version used
\(\tanh(m/0.01)\), which is odd and silently broke the symmetry for negative camber. This is why weights files carry a
feature-version number (currently 2) and old files are refused. A regression test proves the reflection self-test
detects that bug.

## 5. Exact reflection symmetry

Reflecting a section about its chord line, \(\mathcal{R}: (m, p, t, \mathrm{Re}, \alpha) \mapsto (-m, p, t,
\mathrm{Re}, -\alpha)\), must give \(C_L \to -C_L\), \(C_M \to -C_M\) and the same \(C_D\). Write \(\phi(x)\) for the
feature vector and \(S\) for the diagonal sign matrix of the table above. Because the panel constants of the reflected
section give \(C_{L,\mathrm{inv}}(\mathcal{R}x) = -C_{L,\mathrm{inv}}(x)\) and likewise for \(C_M\), and the gate is
even,

\[
\phi(\mathcal{R}x) = S\,\phi(x), \qquad S^2 = I.
\]

With any network \(f = (f_L, f_D, f_M)\), the model outputs

\[
\Delta C_L = \tfrac12[f_L(\phi) - f_L(S\phi)],\quad
\Delta\ln C_D = \tfrac12[f_D(\phi) + f_D(S\phi)],\quad
\Delta C_M = \tfrac12[f_M(\phi) - f_M(S\phi)].
\]

At \(\mathcal{R}x\) these become \(\tfrac12[f_L(S\phi) - f_L(\phi)] = -\Delta C_L\), and similarly for the other two
outputs, for **every** choice of weights. The baselines transform the same way (\(C_{D,0}\) depends only on Re and
\(t\)). Hence

\[
C_L(\mathcal{R}x) = -C_L(x), \qquad C_D(\mathcal{R}x) = C_D(x), \qquad C_M(\mathcal{R}x) = -C_M(x)
\]

holds exactly, up to floating-point round-off. In particular a symmetric section at zero incidence has exactly zero
lift and moment. This is not only argued: every benchmark run executes `neuralnaca.model.check_reflection`, which
compares predictions for 400 random signed-camber conditions with predictions for their reflections, each with its own
panel solve. Property-based tests check the same thing for random networks.

Inside the shipped domain all sections have \(m \ge 0\), so the symmetry mainly acts as a hard constraint around
\(m = 0\), \(\alpha = 0\) (symmetric sections must have antisymmetric polars), where it saves the network from learning
it from data.

## 6. Network and ensemble

* Five independent multilayer perceptrons (7 → 128 → 128 → 128 → 128 → 3, SiLU), evaluated together with batched
  matrix products. Each member is initialised independently and draws its own minibatches.
* Raw outputs are scaled by \((0.1, 1, 0.01)\) for \((C_L, \ln C_D, C_M)\) and added to the baseline
  \((C_{L,\mathrm{inv}}, \ln C_{D,0}, C_{M,\mathrm{inv}})\).
* The prediction is the ensemble mean (for drag, the mean of \(\ln C_D\)). The member spread is reported as
  `*_std`.

## 7. Training

* **Data:** converged XFOIL runs of a scrambled Sobol design over the domain (4,096 runs for the shipped model, of
  which 93 % converged).
* **Loss:** smooth-L1 (Huber, \(\beta = 0.1\)) on \((C_L, \ln C_D, C_M)\) weighted by \((1/0.01, 1/0.03, 1/0.002)\),
  roughly the inverse of the accuracy of interest. The L1 tail makes training robust to the occasional erratic XFOIL
  point near stall.
* **Optimiser:** Adam, one-cycle schedule with peak learning rate \(3\times10^{-3}\), 18,000 steps, batch 512 per
  member.
* **Averaging:** an exponential moving average of the weights (decay 0.9995) over the second half of training
  gives the final model.
* **Optional residuals** (`TrainConfig(residuals=True)`): autograd penalties on 4,096 unlabelled collocation points.
  They are the pre-stall lift slope within [0.5, 1.3] × the inviscid slope, a bound on \(|d^2C_L/d\alpha^2|\), and
  \(d\ln C_D/d\ln\mathrm{Re} \le 0.1\). Each band holds for 97-99 % of XFOIL polar intervals. They do not improve
  accuracy and make training several times slower (both measured in the ablation).

**Provenance of the hyper-parameters.** They were chosen during development on separate XFOIL draws, never on the
benchmark test sets, and frozen before the benchmark. Replications 3 and 4 were generated after the code freeze. The
recipe was not changed after their results were seen.

## 8. Training support

`neuralnaca.support.SupportModel` estimates where the model has training support:

* \(P(\text{XFOIL converges})\) is a distance-weighted vote of the 10 nearest training conditions (converged or not)
  in the unit-scaled input space;
* a prediction is *supported* if it is in the domain, that probability is at least 0.5, and the predicted \(C_D\) does
  not exceed the largest converged training drag.

It is deliberately simple and transparent. Its performance on held-out data is reported in
[Results](results.md#knowing-when-not-to-trust-a-prediction).
